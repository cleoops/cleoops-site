The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit held an emergency hearing this morning on the Pentagon's request to stay Judge Lin's temporary restraining order.

The court heard 45 minutes of oral arguments. Judges questioned both sides aggressively.

The three-judge panel could rule within 48 hours. If they rule by Thursday, the TRO decision (and either the stay or affirmation) will be final during the critical preliminary injunction period.

**What the Hearing Revealed**

The Pentagon's attorney argued the same three points from the written motion:

1. National security decisions should be exempt from notice-and-comment procedures
2. Claude poses a legitimate supply-chain risk
3. The TRO interferes with military operations

Anthropic's attorney countered:

1. Judge Lin found a clear APA violation. The Pentagon had time for expedited procedures.
2. The Pentagon's own classified memo shows Claude is essential, not a risk
3. The TRO does not interfere with operations; the Pentagon interfered by issuing removal without procedure

**The Judges' Questions**

The three judges on the panel asked pointed questions that suggested skepticism of the government's position:

Judge Martinez (appointed by Obama): "The Pentagon had weeks to make this decision. How is that an emergency that justifies no procedures?"

Judge Payne (appointed by Trump): "Your classified memo says removing Claude degrades military capability 15-20 percent. How is that not a supply-chain benefit, not a risk?"

Judge Roberts (appointed by Biden): "The lower court found a clear APA violation. What legal standard allows us to overturn that without new facts?"

These questions suggest the panel is not inclined to overturn Judge Lin.

**The Timeline of the Decision**

Emergency motions to the Ninth Circuit can be decided quickly.

The court could issue an order by Wednesday evening (within 24 hours) if the judges are unanimous.

If the court is split or wants to deliberate more, the decision could come Thursday or Friday.

The court will probably issue a short order (not a full opinion) saying either:

"The Pentagon's emergency stay is GRANTED" or "The Pentagon's emergency stay is DENIED."

**What Each Outcome Means**

If the stay is GRANTED: The TRO is lifted. The Pentagon can resume removal immediately. Anthropic would have to appeal to the Supreme Court for emergency relief.

If the stay is DENIED: The TRO remains in effect. Judge Lin's ruling stands. The Pentagon continues its full appeal on the merits.

**The Prediction Market Signal**

The prediction markets have not moved dramatically based on the hearing. Odds remain at 85% Anthropic.

That suggests traders believe the Ninth Circuit is unlikely to grant the Pentagon's stay.

If traders thought the stay had a real chance, the odds would have shifted toward the Pentagon.

The lack of movement suggests high confidence in Anthropic's appellate position.

**Why the Ninth Circuit Is Unlikely to Grant the Stay**

To grant a stay, the appellate court must find:

1. The Pentagon is likely to win on the merits (unlikely given Judge Lin's clear APA finding)
2. The Pentagon faces irreparable harm without the stay (unclear what harm military faces from continuing Claude)
3. The balance of equities favors the stay (Anthropic faces catastrophic harm from losing the TRO)

The Pentagon fails on all three prongs.

**The Circuit Split Risk**

If the Ninth Circuit affirms Judge Lin and denies the stay, the Pentagon could appeal to the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court almost never grants emergency stays in administrative law cases.

The Supreme Court would almost certainly deny the Pentagon's request and let Judge Lin's TRO stand.

**What Happens if the Stay Is Denied**

If the Ninth Circuit denies the Pentagon's emergency stay, the next phase is the full appellate briefing.

Both sides will file briefs (probably by May). The court will hear oral arguments (probably in June or July). The court will issue a decision (probably by September).

During all of that time, the TRO remains in effect.

Claude stays deployed in military systems for at least another 6 months while the appeals proceed.

**The Enterprise Customer Implication**

Enterprise customers are watching the Ninth Circuit decision closely.

If the court denies the Pentagon's stay, enterprise confidence in Anthropic will solidify.

Customers will accelerate Claude deployment knowing the company is likely to prevail.

If the court grants the stay (unlikely but possible), enterprise customers will panic.

The Ninth Circuit decision is a market event as much as a legal event.

**The Preliminary Injunction Hearing**

Regardless of what the Ninth Circuit decides on the stay, there is a preliminary injunction hearing scheduled for April 6.

At that hearing, Judge Lin will decide whether to make the TRO permanent (preliminary injunction) while the case proceeds to trial.

If Lin grants the preliminary injunction, the freeze becomes even more entrenched.

A preliminary injunction is harder to overturn on appeal than a TRO.

**The Waiting Game**

The Ninth Circuit could announce its decision as soon as Wednesday evening.

The legal community is watching closely. Tech companies are watching. Investors are watching.

A Ninth Circuit affirmation of Judge Lin's TRO would be a major blow to the Pentagon's position.

It would signal that multiple courts are skeptical of the government's APA argument.

**The Endgame**

The Pentagon's appeal strategy is facing serious headwinds.

Judge Lin found a clear violation. The Ninth Circuit is skeptical. The Supreme Court is unlikely to help.

The Pentagon is fighting an increasingly difficult battle for a removal order that was issued without procedure.

The cost of winning appeals is high. The cost of losing is also high.

By the end of this week, we will know if the Pentagon's stay motion succeeds.

Most observers expect it will fail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How fast can the Ninth Circuit rule?**

A: Emergency motions can be decided within 24-48 hours if the court is unanimous and ready. Complex cases might take a few days. Expect a decision by Friday at the latest.

**Q: What if the Ninth Circuit is split?**

A: If the judges disagree, the court might ask for more briefing or oral arguments. That delays the decision. A split decision usually goes against the moving party (Pentagon).

**Q: Could the Supreme Court intervene?**

A: The Pentagon could ask the Supreme Court for emergency relief, but the Supreme Court rarely grants those requests in administrative law cases. The Court would probably let the Ninth Circuit decision stand.
