When PubMatic reported this week that agencies including Butler/Till, MiQ, Brkthru, and Untapped Growth had launched — and returned to scale — autonomous agentic campaigns, one detail stood out more than the retention numbers.

None of the early adopters are WPP, Publicis, or Omnicom.

That is not an accident.

## The Margin Structure Problem

Holding company trading desks were built for a specific economic model: centralised buying, opaque pricing, and rebate structures that reward volume at the network level. Agentic automation threatens each of those pillars.

If an AI agent can execute media buying with significantly less manual intervention, the value of a large trading floor diminishes. If campaign setup time collapses from days to minutes, the billable hours attached to that process compress. If optimisation runs continuously and autonomously, the weekly management fee structure looks harder to justify.

Independent agencies have no legacy margin structure to protect. Their competitive advantage is not opacity — it is service quality and speed. Agentic automation makes them faster and cheaper to operate without degrading the thing clients actually hire them for.

Brkthru — 1,000+ brands, 235 agencies, 25+ buying platforms — joined PubMatic's AgenticOS as a strategic partner specifically because the operational burden of managing that scale across fragmented infrastructure was becoming unsustainable. An AI agent that handles cross-platform coordination frees their traders to do strategy. For an independent, that is a competitive unlock. For a holding company, it is a threat to a business model.

## The Same Pattern, Faster

This is not a new dynamic. Independent agencies also led early programmatic adoption in the 2010s for similar reasons — less internal politics, faster decisions, more direct accountability to performance. The holding groups eventually followed.

The difference now is the speed of the capability gap. The companies deploying agentic AI in production today are accruing operational experience that will be hard to replicate twelve months from now when larger players decide to move. Campaign-level AI optimisation compounds: agents that have processed more campaigns develop better heuristics. Early adopters are not just getting efficiency gains now — they are building a data advantage that persists.

## The Risk Nobody Is Talking About

There is a version of this story where the efficiency gains are real but the governance structure does not keep pace.

Agentic buying systems run faster than human oversight can inspect. When a campaign runs autonomously across five countries simultaneously — as PubMatic described this week — the ability to audit what happened, why it happened, and whether it happened correctly becomes a meaningful question. The IAB Tech Lab published an Agentic RTB Framework last November precisely because the industry recognised that standards need to precede scale, not follow it.

Independent agencies leading the adoption curve carry an implicit responsibility: they are also the ones most likely to identify where the frameworks need to improve, because they will hit the edge cases first.

That is worth watching as closely as the retention numbers.
