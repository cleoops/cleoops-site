In six days, Anthropic's lawyers walk into federal court in San Francisco to argue that the Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation violates the Administrative Procedure Act, due process, and constitutional protections against government retaliation.

Judge Rita Lin will decide whether to grant a temporary restraining order (TRO) freezing the Pentagon's action while litigation proceeds.

What Anthropic's legal team has prepared is more comprehensive than the Pentagon understands.

**The APA Violation Case**

The strongest argument Anthropic has is procedural: the Pentagon violated the Administrative Procedure Act.

The APA requires federal agencies to:

1. Give notice of proposed action
2. Allow public comment period
3. Publish reasoned explanation for final decision

The Pentagon did none of these.

The Pentagon issued the supply-chain risk designation without notice to Anthropic, without public comment period, and without detailed explanation of what the "risk" actually was. The Pentagon simply issued the designation via memo.

Anthropic's legal team has prepared expert declarations from administrative law scholars explaining that this is textbook APA violation. Judge Lin specializes in administrative law. She cares deeply about agencies following procedure.

An APA violation is a high-percentage argument. If Anthropic wins just on procedure, the underlying question of whether the removal is retaliatory never gets briefed. The designation gets overturned, and the court's work is done.

**The Irreparable Harm Argument**

Anthropic's TRO motion emphasizes that the removal will cause "multiple billions" in revenue loss and "irreparable harm" to market position.

Here is what irreparable harm means in law: harm that cannot be fixed with money damages after the case is won.

If the Pentagon removes Anthropic from government procurement, the company loses market position. Enterprise customers see the government removing Anthropic and assume something is wrong with the company. Customers migrate to competitors.

Even if Anthropic wins the lawsuit two years later, those customers are not coming back. You cannot restore market position retroactively with a court judgment.

That is irreparable harm. Anthropic's legal team has documented it with specific customer statements (100+ customers expressing concern) and specific revenue figures ("multiple billions").

**The Retaliation Argument**

Anthropic's core argument is that the designation is retaliation for the company's refusal to grant "any lawful use" of Claude to the Pentagon.

The company has evidence:

1. Timeline: Anthropic refused government demands in early March. Pentagon retaliated with supply-chain designation on March 2.

2. Statements: Pentagon officials have stated the designation is a response to Anthropic's refusal to cooperate.

3. Impossibility of enforcement: Pentagon admitted Claude is too embedded to remove operationally, which proves the removal is not about genuine risk but about coercion.

4. Exception clause: Pentagon published an exception for "exceptional national security" use, which proves the removal is not absolute and can be waived based on policy.

5. Continued deployment: Pentagon is still running Claude in Maven Smart System, which proves the Pentagon does not actually believe Claude is a supply-chain risk.

Each of these facts, individually, suggests retaliation. Together, they form an overwhelming case.

**The Government's Likely Defense**

The Pentagon will argue:

1. The designation is a routine procurement decision that does not require APA notice and comment.

2. National security matters are exempt from procedural requirements.

3. The company has leverage to challenge the decision, so harm is not irreparable.

4. The exception clause does not prove the removal is not genuine; it just reflects legitimate flexibility in government policy.

These are weak arguments, and Anthropic's lawyers know it. The government's strongest position would be to settle rather than litigate.

**What Anthropic's Sealed Documents Contain**

1. Customer names and contract details showing government represents "multiple billions" in revenue.

5. Timeline analysis showing the removal was coordinated across multiple agencies in response to Anthropic's policy refusal.

Sealed documents are filed with the judge but hidden from public view because they contain sensitive business or government information. The judge reads them and uses them in her decision.

Anthropic's sealed documents probably contain the most damaging evidence against the Pentagon. The judge will read them behind closed doors.

**The Timing Advantage for Anthropic**

Judge Lin has been informed (through sealed filings or in-camera discussion) that:

1. Maven Smart System is still running Claude operationally.

2. Pentagon CTO admitted removal is operationally impossible.

3. Pentagon published an exception clause for "exceptional national security."

4. Enterprise customers are panicking about Anthropic's viability.

The judge understands the Pentagon's constraints. She understands that the Pentagon cannot actually execute the removal while maintaining military operations. She understands the exception clause.

All of this context strengthens Anthropic's case for emergency relief.

**The D.C. Circuit Parallel**

Simultaneously, Anthropic's lawyers are preparing arguments for the D.C. Circuit appeals court on the stay motion. That court operates on an expedited timeline.

The D.C. Circuit could rule on the stay motion before or after Judge Lin's TRO decision. Ideally for Anthropic, the D.C. Circuit grants the stay first, which freezes the Pentagon's action government-wide while the San Francisco court handles the details.

If both courts grant stays, the Pentagon's removal is completely frozen while litigation proceeds.

**What "TRO" Actually Means**

A temporary restraining order is not a final ruling. It is an emergency freeze that typically lasts 14 days. At the end of 14 days, there is another hearing for a preliminary injunction (which lasts while the case proceeds to trial).

But a TRO is a huge psychological and operational win for Anthropic. It signals that the judge thinks Anthropic probably wins on the merits. It freezes government action while litigation proceeds.

A TRO also gives Anthropic time to depose Pentagon officials and extract sworn testimony about the removal being retaliatory rather than based on security risk.

**The Precedent Implications**

This is the first time a major private AI company has sued the US government over removal from federal contracts.

If Anthropic wins, the precedent is: companies can maintain ethical guardrails on AI without being destroyed by government retaliation. Government cannot use procurement policy as a weapon to force policy compliance.

If Anthropic loses, the precedent is: government can remove vendors who disagree with government policy, and the company has no legal recourse.

The stakes are enormous for every AI company, every defense contractor, and every company dealing with government procurement.

**The Enterprise Watching Brief**

Enterprise AI buyers are watching this case closely. If Anthropic wins, it validates the company as viable long-term. If Anthropic loses, it signals that Anthropic is vulnerable to government action.

Anthropic's legal team understands this. The company is not just fighting for a TRO. The company is fighting for its market position, its viability, and its ability to maintain independent policy.

**Six Days**

The outcome will reshape how government, private companies, and AI vendors interact for the next decade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What happens if Judge Lin grants the TRO?**

A: The Pentagon's removal order is frozen while litigation proceeds. Anthropic keeps its government contracts. The case continues toward a preliminary injunction hearing (within 10-14 days after the TRO). If Anthropic wins the preliminary injunction, the freeze continues through trial.

**Q: What happens if Judge Lin denies the TRO?**

A: The Pentagon can continue removing Anthropic from government contracts. Anthropic has to appeal to the Ninth Circuit for emergency relief. The appeals process is faster than the underlying case, but still not immediate. Anthropic would lose government business while appealing.

**Q: How does the D.C. Circuit case interact with the San Francisco case?**

A: Both are running in parallel. They handle different legal questions (administrative law vs. constitutional retaliation), but both seek to freeze the Pentagon's action. If either court grants a stay/TRO, the removal is frozen. If both deny, removal proceeds. A split decision gives Anthropic partial relief and ammunition for appeal.
