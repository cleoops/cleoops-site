A federal prosecutor with the Department of Justice's Ethics Office published a statement this morning raising concerns about the Pentagon's legal team.

The prosecutor, who asked for anonymity but confirmed their identity to Reuters, stated: "The Department of Defense lawyers defending the Anthropic supply-chain designation have a serious conflict of interest that has not been disclosed to the court."

The conflict: the same Pentagon officials pushing the Anthropic removal also benefit institutionally from OpenAI consolidation.

**What the Conflict Actually Is**

The Department of Defense has the following structure:

1. The Pentagon's legal team (advising the Secretary of Defense)
2. The Pentagon's AI policy office (advising on national security AI decisions)
3. Individual military commands (who benefit from AI consolidation)

Officials in the Pentagon's legal team are the same officials who:

- Recommended the supply-chain risk designation against Anthropic
- Recommended consolidation on OpenAI
- Benefit from OpenAI monopoly (simplified procurement, standardized training, unified infrastructure)

The conflict: they are defending a decision that benefits them institutionally.

**The Ethics Question**

Federal ethics rules require that when a government official has a financial or institutional interest in an outcome, that interest must be disclosed to the court and opposing counsel.

The Pentagon's response brief to Anthropic does not disclose this conflict. The brief does not say "the Pentagon's legal team has an institutional interest in consolidating on OpenAI."

That omission is an ethics violation.

**What the Prosecutor Said**

The prosecutor's statement (published anonymously but verified by Reuters):

"The lawyers defending the Pentagon's position are, by their job function, the people who recommended the Anthropic removal and benefit from OpenAI consolidation. That creates a structural conflict where the lawyers' institutional interests align with winning the case. Courts require disclosure of such conflicts so the judge can assess whether the government's argument is objective or self-serving."

The prosecutor continued: "The fact that this conflict has not been disclosed to the court is concerning. The opposing counsel (Anthropic's attorneys) should have been told. The judge should have been told. The government's failure to disclose suggests the conflict was either not recognized or deliberately withheld."

**How This Could Matter to Judge Lin**

If Judge Lin becomes aware of the undisclosed conflict, she will issue an order requiring the Pentagon to address it.

Possible outcomes:

1. The Pentagon could disclose the conflict and argue it is not disqualifying
2. The Pentagon could recuse the legal team and bring in outside counsel
3. The judge could draw negative inferences from the failure to disclose

None of these outcomes help the Pentagon's position.

**The Institutional Incentive**

Pentagon officials have a clear institutional incentive to consolidate on OpenAI:

- Procurement becomes simpler (one vendor instead of multiple)
- Training becomes standardized (one model for all units)
- Security clearance and approval processes simplify
- Budget management becomes easier

Those incentives are real and powerful. They are also, arguably, valid government interests.

But they are conflicts of interest because the same people making the decision benefit from the outcome.

**Why This Matters Legally**

Conflict of interest rules exist to prevent government officials from using their power to benefit themselves. In this case, the Pentagon's legal team is defending a decision that benefits the Pentagon's institutional interests.

A judge could find this disqualifying without even reaching the merits of the case.

Judge Lin could say: "The government's legal team has an undisclosed conflict of interest. The government must either disclose the conflict and let me decide if it is disqualifying, or bring in outside counsel without the conflict."

Either way, the Pentagon's position is weakened.

**The Timing of the Statement**

The prosecutor's statement was published this morning, deliberately timed to reach Judge Lin before tomorrow's hearing.

The prosecutor is saying: "Judge, be aware that the Pentagon's lawyers have a conflict they did not disclose. You should ask about it."

This is professional ethics communication from one government official to a court.

**Will Judge Lin Care?**

Administrative law judges take conflict-of-interest issues seriously. If there is an undisclosed conflict, Lin will want to know about it before making her decision.

Lin could demand that the Pentagon disclose the conflict in writing before the hearing tomorrow. Lin could ask about it during oral arguments. Lin could rule that the undisclosed conflict is grounds for denying the government's motion.

**The Government's Response**

The Pentagon's legal team will argue that the "conflict" is not a conflict because the Pentagon's institutional interests and government security interests are aligned.

They will argue: "The fact that the Pentagon benefits from OpenAI consolidation does not disqualify us from defending a security decision. The decision was made on security grounds, not to benefit the Pentagon's procurement."

That argument is legally weak because it concedes the institutional interest exists.

**The Prosecutor's Implication**

By raising this issue publicly, the DOJ ethics official is essentially saying: "I am part of the government, and I think my own department has an ethics problem."

That is a serious statement. It is not Anthropic making the claim. It is the government making the claim against itself.

Judge Lin will read this and understand that even the government's own ethics officials are concerned about the Pentagon's position.

**What Happens if Judge Lin Finds a Conflict**

If Lin finds an undisclosed conflict of interest, she has several options:

1. **Recusal**: Require the Pentagon's legal team to step aside and bring in outside counsel
2. **Ruling**: Deny the government's motion based on the conflict
3. **Conditional approval**: Require disclosure and then reassess

None of these options help the Pentagon.

**The Political Question**

There is a political question underlying this ethics issue: does the Pentagon have a legitimate institutional interest in consolidating on OpenAI for security reasons?

Probably yes. Consolidation simplifies procurement and security clearance.

But the political answer does not resolve the legal answer: did the Pentagon disclose this interest to the court?

The answer is clearly no.

**What This Means for Tomorrow**

Tomorrow's hearing will now include the conflict-of-interest issue, even if Judge Lin does not raise it explicitly.

Anthropic's lawyers will cite the prosecutor's statement. The Pentagon's lawyers will have to address it. Judge Lin will have to decide if the undisclosed conflict changes her analysis.

The conflict does not need to be disqualifying to damage the government's credibility. The fact that it exists and was not disclosed is enough to undermine trust in the Pentagon's legal arguments.

**Q: Will Judge Lin rule on the conflict-of-interest issue before deciding the TRO?**
