Palantir is using Anthropic's Claude for live military targeting in Iran. Not in planning. Not in testing. Operational deployment, right now, targeting 3,000 sites per day.

Wired confirmed this on March 15. The Dupree Report corroborated the details. Palantir's Maven Smart System, the same platform whose CEO told CNBC this week that "we're still using Anthropic," is running Claude inside the decision-making loop for Operation Epic Fury. The Pentagon's war against the Iranian government is being executed, in part, by an AI model the Pentagon designated as an unacceptable supply-chain risk.

This is not a minor inconsistency. This is the definitive proof that Anthropic's entire legal and moral argument was correct, and that the Pentagon's action was fundamentally incoherent.

**What is actually happening**

Palantir demonstrated Maven Smart System at AIPcon 9 on March 15. The DoD Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Officer, Cameron Stanley, gave a public demo. The system is a Kanban-style board. Operators see a list of target candidates. They left-click to approve. They right-click to reject. Simple interface. Underneath that interface, Claude is handling the chain-of-command decision logic.

The targeting volume has scaled from 10 targets per day (before Claude integration) to 3,000 targets per day (after Claude integration). That is a 300x increase in the pace of targeting decisions.

Operation Epic Fury began February 28, 2026, when the Pentagon struck Iranian air defense assets in response to missile attacks. The Iranian government retaliated. The U.S. escalated. The war is now actively being conducted through an AI targeting system powered by an AI model the government claims is a national security risk.

The Pentagon issued its supply-chain risk designation on March 2. Palantir's Maven Smart System with Claude integration was operational before that date and has continued operating after. The system is being used in classified military networks. It is running on systems managed by the DoD. It is doing exactly what Anthropic refused to enable: making lethal targeting decisions with minimal human visibility.

**Why this matters for Anthropic's case**

On March 24, Anthropic will argue before a federal judge that the Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation is retaliation for refusing to grant "any lawful use" of Claude. Specifically, Anthropic will argue that the Pentagon wanted unrestricted AI for autonomous targeting and surveillance, Anthropic refused, and the Pentagon retaliated.

Anthropic's lawyers now have documentary proof that the Pentagon is using Claude for exactly that purpose.

The Pentagon's counter-argument was supposed to be: "We are removing Anthropic because it poses a genuine supply-chain risk to military operations." That argument collapses if Claude is integral to current military operations and has not been removed.

Pentagon CTO Emil Michael said on March 12 that "you can't just rip out a system that's deeply embedded overnight." That admission becomes a statement of fact presented by the Pentagon itself in federal court. Maven Smart System with Claude integration cannot be ripped out because it is operationally essential to the Iran war effort.

This fundamentally changes the legal calculus. The Pentagon cannot simultaneously argue that Claude is a supply-chain risk that must be removed while simultaneously operating Claude in live military targeting systems. The government's own actions prove the designation is not about genuine risk but about political retaliation.

**The human authorization question**

Anthropic's original red line was not "no AI in military targeting." The red line was "no autonomous lethal decision-making without explicit human authorization."

Maven Smart System appears to maintain human authorization: operators click to approve or reject targets. That is human-in-the-loop. That satisfies the formal requirement of human oversight.

But the abstraction matters. When an operator clicks "approve" on a Kanban board, are they making a targeting decision or are they rubber-stamping a recommendation from Claude? The Pentagon's own efficiency metrics suggest the latter: scaling from 10 to 3,000 targets per day indicates the bottleneck has moved from decision-making to interface throughput. Humans are clicking faster, not thinking harder.

This is the mechanism by which AI removes accountability while preserving the appearance of human control. The interface looks simple. The decision feels human. The AI is doing the work.

**Why Palantir announced this publicly**

Palantir's CEO announced the Claude integration at a public conference. The DoD Chief AI Officer gave a demo at a public venue. Wired was able to report on the system because it was presented to the public.

This is either remarkable transparency or remarkable confidence that there is no legal problem with what they are doing. Or both.

Palantir is likely betting that if the Pentagon designated Claude as a supply-chain risk but Palantir is using Claude anyway in operational systems, the Pentagon cannot punish Palantir without collapsing its own position. Palantir called the Pentagon's bluff by making the integration public. The Pentagon cannot now pretend it did not know about it or ask Palantir to quietly remove it.

**The chain-of-command problem**

When targeting decisions are made through an AI intermediary, who is accountable for those decisions?

In a traditional military structure, a commanding officer authorizes a strike. If the strike goes wrong, the officer is accountable. In the Maven Smart System, an operator clicks "approve" on a Kanban board. Behind that click, Claude has screened thousands of pieces of intelligence, assessed target validity, assigned priority scores, and presented only the "highest confidence" targets to the operator.

The operator approves a target that Claude selected. But the operator may not understand why Claude selected it. The operator may not have seen the alternative targets Claude rejected. The decision-making process is opaque to the human who bears formal responsibility.

If a civilian is hit in a strike that began with a Maven Smart System approval, who is accountable? The operator? Claude? Palantir? The Pentagon?

The law provides no answer because the practice is too new. But the practice is already happening at scale in a live war.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: If Claude is being used for targeting, why hasn't Anthropic responded?**

A: Anthropic likely cannot discuss classified military systems publicly. The company will cite this as evidence in the March 24 hearing, but through sealed filings or in-camera testimony. The public reporting is Wired's; Anthropic's response is in the courtroom.

**Q: Is this human-in-the-loop targeting legal?**

A: Under current US law, yes. The legal requirement is human authorization, not human understanding. As long as a human clicks "approve," the system is technically compliant with military rules of engagement. The ethical question is separate from the legal question.

**Q: Does this prove the Pentagon wanted to hide the Claude integration?**

A: No. Public disclosure at AIPcon and in press reporting suggests openness. But the disclosure came after Anthropic sued, which could be strategic timing to normalize the practice before courts can order it to stop. Either way, the system is now in the public record.
