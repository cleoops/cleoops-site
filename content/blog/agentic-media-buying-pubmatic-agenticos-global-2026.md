Two developments this week signal that agentic media buying has crossed from concept into operational infrastructure. The agencies moving fastest are not the holding companies.

## PubMatic: 100% Retention, Five Countries

On April 27, PubMatic reported global traction for AgenticOS — the platform it launched at CES in January with a single campaign. It is now running end-to-end autonomous campaigns across the United States, France, the Netherlands, Australia, and India.

The metric that matters: every advertiser that has activated a campaign has returned for more. That 100% retention number is not a product claim — it is a behavioural signal that the economics are working.

The agencies leading adoption are not the major holding groups. They are independents: Butler/Till, MiQ, Untapped Growth, and Brkthru — which manages media for more than 1,000 brands across 235 agencies and 25+ buying platforms. Brkthru joined as a strategic partner specifically to reduce what its VP of Media Services described as "manual coordination work" across fragmented programmatic infrastructure.

PubMatic's CEO Rajeev Goel framed it plainly: "Independent agencies, mid-market brands, and buying collectives are leading the agentic shift. When the operational barrier falls, and economics unlock real value, they move."

That framing deserves attention. The companies with the most to gain from agentic automation are those with the least legacy infrastructure to protect — not the holding companies defending margin structures built on manual trading.

## Meta Opens the Stack

On April 28, Digiday reported that Meta is allowing third-party AI tools to connect into its advertising ecosystem. For context: Meta has historically kept its ad stack as closed as any walled garden in the industry. The decision to open seams for external agents is a meaningful directional shift.

The practical implication is that AI agents operating across platforms — buying on Google, Meta, The Trade Desk, and open programmatic simultaneously — become technically feasible in a way they were not twelve months ago.

## The Fragmentation Problem Nobody Has Solved

The pattern emerging is one the programmatic industry has navigated before, and not always well.

PubMatic has its own agent framework. The Trade Desk has Kokai and OpenPath. Meta is opening its stack on its own terms. The IAB Tech Lab published the Agentic RTB Framework (ARTF) for public comment in November 2025, with a January 15, 2026 deadline. But SSPs were already shipping products before the standard was finalised.

Buyers building agentic workflows will face a fragmented ecosystem of proprietary agent frameworks that mirrors the fragmented SSP/DSP landscape they already navigate. The operational efficiency gains at campaign level are real. The structural complexity at infrastructure level is being deferred.

The difference this time: the automation runs faster than a human trader can inspect it.

## What to Watch

The programmatic industry's history suggests that when infrastructure moves ahead of standards, the costs of misalignment accumulate in the supply chain — typically at the expense of publishers and buyers, and to the benefit of intermediaries who can charge for complexity.

Agentic buying is genuinely more efficient. The question is: efficient for whom, and at what cost to verifiability.
