The National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) has been working with Anthropic since February 2024 on one of the most consequential research problems in national security: how to defend the United States against artificial intelligence that could be weaponized to design nuclear weapons.

Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory deployed Claude to 10,000 scientists in 2025 as a tool for research spanning "nuclear deterrence, energy security, and materials science." The research partnership was explicitly designed to understand the risks of frontier AI in a domain where errors measure in megatons.

This week, the Trump administration's push to remove Anthropic from all federal agencies is now threatening to terminate that research.

The irony is so sharp it cuts through the noise of the entire Pentagon-Anthropic controversy: the government is removing the AI company built on safety principles from the agency responsible for defending against unsafe AI. That is not a vendor dispute. That is an own-goal in the middle of a geopolitical competition.

**The research that is now at risk**

The NNSA partnership with Anthropic is explicitly described as research into "how AI models could potentially be misused to develop nuclear and radiological threats." In plain language: the government hired Anthropic to build tools that would help detect when AI is being used to design weapons of mass destruction.

This is the opposite of the Pentagon's "any lawful use" mandate. This is the government paying Anthropic to be paranoid about how AI could be weaponized. This is exactly what Anthropic's founders designed the company to do: anticipate failure modes before they metastasize into catastrophe.

Lawrence Livermore has 10,000 scientists. They conduct research in weapons effects, radiation transport, physics simulation, and the fundamental science of nuclear weapons. Claude is integrated into their daily work: helping them analyze papers, run simulations, debug code, propose hypotheses. That integration has been in place for 12 months.

Removing Claude from that 10,000-person research institution is not a minor contract termination. It is a forced interruption of ongoing classified research at the most critical national security laboratory in the country.

**Why this matters for national security**

China is not removing its AI partners from nuclear weapons research. China is subsidizing OpenClaw development and encouraging local governments to fund AI agent deployment. China is running its own frontier AI labs and studying the intersection of AI and weapons technology. While the Pentagon pushes Anthropic out, China's labs are doubling down on the same AI capabilities.

The NNSA partnership with Anthropic existed for one reason: to stay ahead of China in understanding how AI could be weaponized. The Pentagon's action eliminates the research specifically designed to maintain that advantage.

The government's framing of Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" assumes the risk is coming from Anthropic. The real risk is China. The real vulnerability is the U.S. government's own lack of AI expertise in nuclear security. Removing Anthropic does not reduce that vulnerability; it deepens it.

For weapons strategists: imagine if the Pentagon had removed nuclear physicists from national labs during the Cold War because of a policy dispute with a university. That is the equivalent of what is happening now. The infrastructure of American nuclear defense is being damaged by a political fight that has nothing to do with nuclear defense.

**The 10,000-scientist consequence**

Lawrence Livermore's 10,000 scientists are going to lose access to a tool they have been using daily for 12 months. That is not a mild inconvenience. That is operational disruption to classified research.

The NNSA's statement that it is "reviewing all existing contracts" with Anthropic means the partnership is almost certainly being terminated. The Energy Department did not get a phone call asking it to comply with the Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation. It got a political directive: remove Anthropic.

When you remove a tool that 10,000 scientists have been using for a year, you create a productivity cliff. The scientists will have to migrate to alternative tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, or custom models). That migration takes months. The research during the migration period slows down.

More critically: the research into "how AI could be used to design nuclear weapons" stops. The NNSA had a project team dedicated to that work. That team is now dissolved. The research questions that team was working on remain unanswered. China's research into the same questions continues uninterrupted.

**The precedent this sets**

If the government can remove a company from nuclear weapons research because of a policy disagreement unrelated to the research, then every contractor relationship in classified work becomes vulnerable to political purges.

Think about what this precedent means for the next crisis. A different administration. A different AI company. Different policy disagreements. The mechanism is now established: disagree with the government, get removed from classified work, watch your research projects get terminated, watch geopolitical advantage shift to China.

No private company will invest in national security work if the payoff is that a political dispute ends the contract overnight. The weapons labs will be forced to build everything in-house with government staff, which is slower, more expensive, and less innovative than vendor partnerships.

The Pentagon did not think about this when it pushed the supply-chain risk designation. The Pentagon was fighting a proxy war with Anthropic over surveillance authority. The nuclear weapons lab was collateral damage.

**What happens now**

NNSA told Fast Company it is "reviewing all existing contracts" with Anthropic. That review will almost certainly result in termination. The Energy Department will either cancel the contract or wind it down over 90-180 days.

Lawrence Livermore's 10,000 scientists will lose Claude. They will petition for exceptions. The Pentagon will refuse. They will be told to migrate to OpenAI or Google. The research into AI-weaponization defenses will be delayed.

Meanwhile: China's labs continue working. Tsinghua University, Shanghai AI Lab, and others are running research into the intersection of AI and nuclear weapons. They have no policy constraints. They have government funding. They have geopolitical motivation.

The U.S. falls behind.

**Why this angle is not being covered**

The Anthropic story dominates headlines as a Pentagon contract dispute, a government overreach story, or a political drama. The nuclear safety research angle is buried in a paragraph of the Fast Company piece that announced it.

Why? Because it is not sensational. "NNSA partnership continues" is not news. "NNSA partnership terminated due to Pentagon politics" is important, but the connection to nuclear security is not obvious to most readers.

But for anyone who understands the stakes of AI weapons, this is the most damaging consequence of the Pentagon's action. It is the consequence that compounds over time. In 2027, when China's AI labs have made advances in nuclear weapons simulation that the U.S. is not tracking, the Pentagon will wonder why it was caught flat-footed.

The answer will be that they removed the one company specifically built to anticipate that problem.

**The cost of principle**

Anthropic refused to grant the Pentagon "any lawful use" of Claude. The Pentagon retaliated. Anthropic sued. The government escalated. The Enterprise market divided over vendor choice. The defense contractor ecosystem was forced to choose between government demands and operational reality.

But the hidden cost — the cost that will not appear in quarterly earnings reports or headlines — is the nuclear safety research that stopped this week.

The government is in the middle of a lawsuit with a company that refused to let the government use AI for mass surveillance. The government is winning that fight (from a coercive power perspective). But in winning it, the government is losing research that defends against AI-enabled nuclear weapons.

That is not a trade-off. That is an own-goal.

**What you should do about it**

If you are an enterprise AI buyer evaluating Anthropic: understand that the government just demonstrated it can terminate a research partnership unilaterally for policy reasons. That risk extends to all enterprise customers. Anthropic's safety principles might make the government angry enough to destroy the company's ability to serve anyone, government or enterprise.

That is not a reason to avoid Anthropic. It is a reason to understand the risk and factor it into your vendor calculus.

If you are a government contractor: you are watching Palantir openly defy the Anthropic ban and the Pentagon admit it cannot enforce it. But you are also watching the Energy Department terminate nuclear weapons research due to the same dispute. The risks cut both ways.

If you are a policy maker: you just learned that removing a vendor from one domain (Pentagon) has consequences in another domain (nuclear safety research) that you did not anticipate. The government's AI policy is not coordinated across agencies. That is a structural vulnerability that will get worse as AI becomes more critical to more agencies.

**The arc to watch**

The Anthropic lawsuit goes to court on March 24. Everyone is watching to see if Anthropic wins the legal case. But the real outcome is already happening: the government is removing Anthropic from its most important research.

The Pentagon will write that off as acceptable cost.

History will judge it as the moment the U.S. fell behind in defending against AI-weaponization.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Lawrence Livermore actually losing access to Claude?**

A: NNSA told Fast Company it is "reviewing all existing contracts" with Anthropic. In government speak, that means the contract is being terminated. The review will not reverse the decision; it will just establish the timeline. Expect access to be removed within 90 days.

**Q: Can the NNSA just keep using Claude even if the Pentagon bans it?**

A: Not without explicit exception. The Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation applies government-wide. Individual agencies can request exceptions, but NNSA has not done so. The agency is in full compliance mode, not resistance mode.

**Q: Is China actually ahead in AI weapons research?**

A: Unknown. But the U.S. government is about to stop monitoring the question. When you terminate the research designed to detect threats in a domain, you also lose visibility into what those threats are. That is the worst position to be in during a geopolitical competition.
