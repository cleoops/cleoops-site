Anthropic filed an emergency motion in the D.C. Circuit appeals court asking for a stay of the Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation pending judicial review. The company's dollar figure is unambiguous: the removal will cost "hundreds of millions, or even multiple billions of dollars" in lost revenue for 2026.

That is not hyperbole from a lawyer. That is the company's formal declaration of financial impact, filed in federal court.

This filing arrives the same day the Pentagon issued its formal 180-day removal memo. Anthropic's legal team saw the memo, understood it as operational escalation, and immediately filed for emergency relief.

**Three Grounds for the Stay Motion**

Anthropic is arguing:

1. **Likelihood of success on the merits**: The supply-chain designation violates the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) because it was issued without notice, without a public comment period, and without reasoned explanation. Courts hate procedural shortcuts in administrative action. If Anthropic wins on APA grounds alone, the designation is overturned.

2. **Irreparable harm**: The government cannot undo the damage of removing Anthropic from federal procurement. Once customers switch to OpenAI or Google, they're locked in by integration costs. Enterprise relationships take years to build and seconds to destroy. The harm is irreparable because money damages won't restore market position.

3. **Balance of equities favors Anthropic**: The Pentagon's interest in removing Anthropic is political (Anthropic refused "any lawful use" on AI for mass surveillance). Anthropic's interest in maintaining government relationships is commercial and existential. The court should weigh these interests and decide the balance favors staying the order while it reviews the underlying validity.

**The $Billions Claim**

Anthropic is claiming revenue loss in the billions for 2026. That's the clearest signal yet of how much government business the company does.

For context: Anthropic's total funding to date is $5.3 billion (through Series C in 2024). The company was valued at $15 billion in its last funding round. If government contracts represent "multiple billions" in 2026 revenue, the government is not a customer—it's the entire business.

That claim makes the case for emergency relief stronger. The court now understands that the Pentagon's action is existentially threatening to Anthropic's viability, not a marginal business disruption.

**The 100-Customer Signal**

Anthropic's filing notes that 100+ enterprise customers have already reached out expressing concern about continuing relationships with a company being systematically removed from federal procurement.

That's the cascade the Pentagon wanted to trigger. By removing Anthropic from government, the Pentagon hoped to delegitimize Anthropic in the enterprise market. The filing proves that strategy is working in the short term—enterprise customers are panicking.

But the filing also signals that Anthropic is documenting the damage. If the court rules in Anthropic's favor, the company will have a documentary record of how many customers expressed concern because of the Pentagon's action. That's evidence of retaliation and damages.

**Why the D.C. Circuit Now**

Anthropic is litigating on two coasts:

- **California**: Federal court in San Francisco (Judge Rita Lin). TRO hearing March 24 for the federal lawsuit against the Pentagon.

- **D.C. Circuit**: Emergency stay motion on the administrative law question of whether the Pentagon's supply-chain designation is valid.

The D.C. Circuit is the fastest-moving front because it operates on an expedited timeline for administrative law cases. Judges understand that government agencies can do enormous damage while litigation is pending. The stay motion asks the court to freeze government action while the underlying question is resolved.

**The Pentagon's Likely Response**

The Pentagon's response brief (expected within 10 days) will probably argue:

1. The supply-chain designation is a normal procurement decision that does not require administrative notice and comment.

2. National security matters are exempt from procedural requirements like the APA.

3. The designation is not retaliation but a legitimate security assessment of Anthropic's refusal to cooperate on government priorities.

That last argument is weak given that the Pentagon's own CTO admitted Claude is "deeply embedded" in military operations and that removal is operationally difficult. If the system is so embedded that it cannot be removed without harming national security, it cannot simultaneously be a "supply-chain risk" that is easily eliminated.

**The Court's Dilemma**

If the D.C. Circuit grants the stay, it's essentially saying: "We think Anthropic probably wins. Freeze the designation while we decide."

If it denies the stay, the Pentagon can continue removal while the case proceeds. But the court is then allowing the government to execute its contested action while that action is being challenged as potentially illegal.

Courts generally avoid the second scenario because it makes litigation meaningless. If the government can do whatever it wants while being sued, the lawsuit becomes academic.

So there's a real possibility the D.C. Circuit grants the stay.

**The Timeline Matters**

March 24 is the San Francisco TRO hearing. That's when Judge Lin decides whether to freeze the Pentagon's action locally.

The D.C. Circuit will rule on the stay motion within 2-4 weeks, probably.

If both courts grant stays, the Pentagon's removal order is frozen while litigation proceeds.

If both deny, Anthropic is forced to litigate while the removal is actively happening.

If they split (one grants, one denies), Anthropic gets partial relief and can argue the split is evidence that the designation is vulnerable.

**What Anthropic Is Doing Right**

Anthropic's legal strategy is disciplined:

1. **Document everything**: The filing cites the exact business impact (government represents "multiple billions" in revenue), the exact customer impact (100+ customers expressing concern), and the exact timeline (removal happening while lawsuit is pending).

2. **Hit procedural weaknesses**: The APA violation (no notice, no comment, no reasoned explanation) is a high-percentage argument. Courts are trained to enforce procedure. If Anthropic wins just on procedure, the substantive question never reaches a judge.

3. **Litigate simultaneously**: California federal court for the underlying retaliation claim. D.C. Circuit for the administrative law question. Two judges, two jurisdictions, two opportunities to win. The Pentagon has to defend both.

4. **Frame as company-destroying**: By emphasizing that the Pentagon's action threatens company viability ("multiple billions" in revenue loss), Anthropic is arguing for emergency relief. Courts take existential harm seriously. Marginal business loss does not get stays; company-threatening loss does.

**Enterprise Buyers Are Watching**

The outcome of the D.C. Circuit stay motion will be a signal to every enterprise buyer of how safe it is to use Anthropic.

If the court grants the stay, Anthropic looks vindicated and strengthened.

If the court denies the stay, Anthropic looks vulnerable and customers will accelerate their migration to competitors.

This is the moment the legal outcome directly shapes market perception and adoption.

**What Success Looks Like for Anthropic**

Ideal outcome: D.C. Circuit grants the stay. San Francisco court grants the TRO. Both courts freeze the Pentagon's action while litigation proceeds. Anthropic survives the spring/summer of 2026 with government business intact, can point to vindication in court, and regains enterprise customer confidence.

Realistic outcome: Mixed results across the courts. Some government business is lost, but enough is preserved that Anthropic survives. Enterprise customers pause but don't abandon. The company fights litigation for 2-3 more years while rebuilding market position.

Worst case: Both courts deny relief. Pentagon removal proceeds. Enterprise customers accelerate migration. Anthropic becomes a smaller, focused company without government scale. The company survives but with dramatically reduced market position.

The D.C. Circuit stay motion decision will determine which scenario happens.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What does "irreparable harm" actually mean in law?**

A: It means the harm cannot be fixed with money. If you lose $100M in government contracts, you can be compensated after the case is won. But if you lose enterprise market position because the Pentagon removed you, that market position cannot be restored with a court judgment later. It's permanent damage to reputation and relationships. That's irreparable harm.

**Q: Could Anthropic settle with the Pentagon instead of fighting in court?**

A: Legally, yes. Realistically, the Pentagon has shown no interest in settlement. The Pentagon issued formal removal orders WHILE Anthropic was already suing. That's a sign the Pentagon wants total removal, not compromise. Settlement is unlikely unless political pressure forces the Pentagon to back down.

**Q: How fast can the D.C. Circuit rule on the stay motion?**

A: Usually 2-4 weeks for an expedited motion. Could be faster (days) if the court considers it truly emergency. Could be slower (weeks) if the Pentagon's response is complicated. The court will want both sides to brief the issue, so expect at least 10 days for briefing, then another 5-10 days for ruling.
